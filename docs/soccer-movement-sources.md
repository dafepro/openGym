# Soccer movement starter sessions

The ten sessions in `training/src/data/movement-workouts.js` are original editorial starter sessions, not individual trainer assignments or reproductions of federation programmes. Their exact layouts, sets, repetitions, durations, rest periods and easier options are suggested starting points. The references below establish coaching themes and principles; they do not prescribe these exact workouts or imply endorsement. The player’s age and current training level are unknown, so the sessions use controlled movement, walking alternatives and optional reductions in work. No session is a maximal fitness test, rehabilitation prescription or injury-prevention programme.

Every drill explicitly marks media as unavailable. Source pages may contain their own media; none has been downloaded, licensed, approved or represented as playable in this library.

Sources checked on 2026-10-30. The large FIFA Youth Football PDF exceeded the web reader’s size limit; its indexed printed page 140 was available and used for the limited endurance principles identified below.

| Session | Coaching basis and adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Agility: smooth changes of direction | [FIFA, Agility training for better ball control](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/4-to-8/agility-training-for-better-ball-control.php) offers slalom movement and simplification by reducing cones. Here it becomes a solo walking/jogging course without a race. [England Football, Movement skills](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles-and-resources/coaching/resources/2025/How-to-develop-your-players-movement-skills) provides the wider agility and deceleration theme. |
| Coordination: feet, arms and rhythm | [FIFA, Coordination and the game, warm-up section](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/test/FIFA_Session_Plan_Grassroots_049.pdf) encourages varied physical activities and removing competition to concentrate on movement. Marching, gates and side-step sequences are original simple implementations. |
| Acceleration: build speed and slow smoothly | [FIFA, Defensive anticipation](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/talent-coach-programme/press-and-regain/passing-circuit-defensive-anticipation.php) identifies controlled acceleration and deceleration in pressing. These solo build-up and slowing-zone exercises isolate that theme without the source’s opposition or contact. They use moderate pace rather than maximum speed. |
| Endurance: easy movement intervals | [FIFA Youth Football, printed p. 140, hosted by Irish FA](https://www.irishfa.com/media/14670/fifa-youth-football.pdf) discusses individual pacing and talking during gentle/moderate running. The walking/jogging routes and brief intervals are editorial, with no heart-rate or fitness-test targets. |
| Ball endurance: move at an easy rhythm | [FIFA Youth Football, printed p. 140](https://www.irishfa.com/media/14670/fifa-youth-football.pdf) discusses integrating the ball into sustained activity. These easy ball routes are original; they prioritise comfortable pacing and control rather than speed or touch counts. |
| Balance: steady support and controlled steps | [England Football, Movement skills](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles-and-resources/coaching/resources/2025/How-to-develop-your-players-movement-skills) identifies balance among the fundamental movement skills. [FIFA, Bring all the elements into the game](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/4-to-8/bring-all-the-elements-into-the-game.php) illustrates combining balance, coordination and agility. The holds, short reaches and stepping tasks are original low-impact alternatives, not copies of its chase game. |
| Mobility: an easy movement warm-up | [FIFA, Dynamic Mobility](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/training-perspectives/athleticism-for-young-players/athleticism-for-young-players-dynamic-mobility.php) emphasises purposeful, controlled movement. These standing exercises simplify the theme; they do not reproduce the floor-based sequence. [FIFA, Optimising warm-ups](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/grassroots-and-youth-football-essentials/grassroots-coaching-essentials/optimising-warm-ups-u12-to-u15.php) supports gradual general movement before specific work. Its age band is not claimed for this unpersonalised library. |
| Defending: lateral steps and recovery routes | [The FA, Introducing defending](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/introducing-defending) emphasises patience and effective movement in individual defending. The library rehearses approach, sideways movement and returning to a goal gate without tackling or opposition. |
| Reaction: read a partner and move | [FIFA, Teambuilding, warm-up section](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/test/FIFA_Session_Plan_Grassroots_033.pdf) describes partner movement games and a walking simplification. The original mirror, pointing and traffic-light tasks use separate spaces and avoid tagging. |
| Mixed conditioning: an easy soccer circuit | [FIFA, Orientation and the game, warm-up section](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/test/FIFA_Session_Plan_Grassroots_074.pdf) offers varied courses and lower-intensity adaptations. [FIFA, Coordination and the game](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/test/FIFA_Session_Plan_Grassroots_049.pdf) supports varying movement tasks. This circuit uses walking, easy jogging, lateral movement and a ball route, with the pacing principle from FIFA Youth Football. |

Shared warm-up and closing walks are editorial organisation choices. Warm-ups start with walking before gentle movement. Optional pacing, extra rest and easier alternatives are written into the usable drill instructions. `side: 'each'` is used only for balance tasks that explicitly refer to the displayed support leg, so completing left and right does not double an instruction that already asks for both sides. Partner reaction tasks use two sets with a role swap; the logged activity describes the pair’s complete block.
